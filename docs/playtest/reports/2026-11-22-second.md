# Playtest report: second (2026-11-22)

Seed 2024, 512x512, landing 324,268 level 225. 36 turns, day 23 13:00. Game folder `playtests/second` (notes in `notes.md`).

## 1. Outcome

- **Day 23.** People: 7 founders + 18 settlers = 25. 0 born, 0 left, 0 died. Mood 60%, homeless 0, nobody starving.
- **Food:** 803 meals = 10.7 days. Bread 74 (oven running since day 11), glowcaps 184 (underground farm), grain 520, berries 324.
- **Built:** an underground workshop complex at level 220 (stair 324,266): Textile Hall (spinning wheels x2, loom, sewing table), Carpentry (joinery benches, later demolished), Kilnworks (charcoal clamp), Kitchen (millstone + oven), Scriptorium (paper vat, colour mill, writing desk), Academy (lectern), 22 glowcap plots. On the surface: 48 wheat plots, stockpiles (496 cells), three homes: Home #1 Cottage (90 cells, 22 beds, 21 residents), Home #8 House (6 beds, 2), Home #9 Cottage (7 beds, 2). All stations are size 1.
- **Industry:** planks, rough furniture, tableware, straw pallets, rushlights, thread, cloth, woven rugs, clothing (2), charcoal, flour, bread, paper, ink, pigments and books all made. **Education works:** 4 new Literates (days 15, 18, 20, 23). No metal: I found no ore (see Friction). No tier-2 home goods (each needs cloth in volume or bronze).
- **Living standard** is 0% at the end. It peaked at 15% on day 13 and drifted down while the homes filled.

## 2. Timeline

- D1: stockpile, chop, gather, wheat farm, stair 324,266 down to 220, 12x8 hall dig. 191 logs in 12h.
- D1-3: **lost about 1.5 days.** The stair touches the hall only diagonally (324,266 to 323,265), so 95 digs were unreachable and everyone idled at 44-73%. I used up 3 turns on `view` to find out why. Fixed with one dig at 323,266.
- D3-4: rooms painted while the dig was still running only covered dug cells (Textile Hall got 1 cell). Repainted.
- D4-5: straw pallets were going into Home #1 within 12h of the sewing table being built. 4 beds/4 residents at D5 01:00, 7/7 at D5 13:00. **The home-furnishing fix works.**
- D6: wheat harvested (grain 188). Carpentry output crates got clogged with planks, which blocked rough furniture. I demolished the saw bench.
- D7: 2 settlers ("drawn by its food and free beds"), glowcaps 82.
- D8-10: **7 crate blueprints in kitchen, kiln and textiles stayed unbuilt for 36h.** Once I re-placed them on cells with no loose goods, all were built within 12h.
- D11: **first bread.** D13: first books. D15: **first Literate** (Dayn).
- D17-20: two marriages, each couple moving into a new home that was furnished within 24h. 24 people by D19 (5 settlers "drawn by its happy people").
- D19-23: growth stalls at 24-25. More than 1800 items lie loose, hauling takes 42-47%, and chains wait on inputs that are listed "in stock".

## 3. Pacing

- **First day:** fast. Logs flood in (191 in 12h, 307 by day 2). Berries and bread give a 13-day cushion, so food never worried me. The only slowdown was my stair mistake, which the game didn't help me find.
- **First week:** about right once the hall existed. Stations and pallets appear within a turn and homes fill within a day. Wheat in 4 days and glowcaps in 3 feel good. Mood fell from 50% to 22% in the first 2 days while everyone was homeless, then recovered steadily once beds arrived. The first wave of settlers came on day 3 even at 22% mood.
- **After day 8:** each new chain took 1-3 days of babysitting crates. Charcoal waited a day on logs while 130+ logs sat in stock, and the academy waited a day for paper. From day 19, hauling decides everything. With 25 people and 11 free beds, no settlers came on days 20-22 except one. Education is slow but steady with a size-1 lectern: one Literate about every 2.5 days.

## 4. Friction

- **Unreachable orders give no signal.** "Open orders: Dig 95" while 7 people stood idle. A miner had dug 323,265 diagonally, so I assumed the route was fine. I expected "Dig 95 (95 unreachable)" or idle colonists saying why.
- **The level view draws dug floor as `#`**, the same symbol as solid rock (`view 324 266 220`: the whole dug hall is `#`; probe says Air). I thought the hall had caved in. Probe only shows from standing level+2 down to about 30 tiles, so it couldn't show me the top of the stair shaft either.
- **`view` costs a turn.** Each 1-hour view turn counts against the turn budget. A free `view` (like `probe`) would help.
- **Crates belong to the room, not to a machine, and the first product claims a crate.** This came up four times:
  - The saw bench filled every new output crate with planks (321,260 at T10, 322,260 at T14). Rough furniture then had "output crates full".
  - Flour took the crate I meant for bread (320,256).
  - Cloth ended up in an input crate (313,259).
  - The status line "needs 2 output crates (one per product)" was the only clue to how this works.
  - What I expected: a crate shows which item it is for and can be pinned to one (`part output_crate X Y item=bread`).
- **No production targets and no pause.** By day 9 I had 21 straw pallets, 23 rough furniture and 42 tableware while the homes wanted none. The writing desk used every sheet of paper on 5 books while the lectern starved for paper. The sewing table took the cloth meant for books. My only tools were demolishing a station or setting a recipe it can't make (`recipe 305 258 treatises`) to idle it. I expected "make until N in stock" (DF work orders / RimWorld "do until X").
- **Priorities are blunt.** Construction 1 for everyone still gave "building 0-1%" while a gather order was open. Hauling 1 for everyone built 17 parts in 12h but dropped factory work to 0%. Material delivery seems to count as hauling.
- **"In stock" includes goods lying where they were gathered.** There were 146-174 fiber "in stock", yet the paper vat was "waiting for fiber" and Dayn was "fetching fiber at (366,315)", 45 tiles away.
- **"Living standard" is unexplained.** It went 0% (D1-12), 15% (D13), 11%, 7%, 3%, 1%, then 0% (D23), even though all tier-1 home wants were met by day 19.
- **Merged homes.** A home painted next to another merges into it (Home #1 became 60, then 90 cells). That's fine, but the order reply doesn't say so.
- **Policy change shrinks a home.** Switching Home #8 to House changed 7 beds to "room for 6". The 7 straw pallets already installed counted as nothing toward its new tier-2 wants.

## 5. Bugs and oddities

1. **Blueprints on cells with loose goods never get built** (T9-T19).
   - What happened: crates at 315,258 and 314,260 (textiles), then 314,254 (kiln) and 318,256, 319,256, 319,254 (kitchen), plus 316,260, 314,261 and 315,261, all said "BLUEPRINT (needs 2 logs and a builder)" for 24-48h. Logs were 160-300 and everyone had construction 1. The level map showed `*` goods on or around those cells (dig rubble, glowcap harvest).
   - Evidence: I cancelled them and placed the same parts on cells the map showed as clean (`part input_crate 314 255 z=220`, etc.). All 7 were built within 12h. The machines in the same orders were built, probably because their cells happened to be clean.
   - Expected: haulers clear the cell, or the status says "blocked by goods on the cell".
2. **The first product claims an output crate** (T10-T14, Carpentry 318..323,258..261). The saw bench's planks filled output crates added for the joinery bench (321,260: 4, then 12, then 40 planks; 322,260: 16 planks within 12h of being built). Status: "output crates full", with rough furniture wanted by the home and none made. Same with flour in the bread crate 320,256 (T21).
3. **Rough furniture's crate was taken by tableware** (T15). Crate 323,260 held 1 rough_furniture, then "holds 0 wooden_ware". An emptied crate goes to whichever product reaches it next, so rough furniture lost its only crate again.
4. **Stockpile size had no effect on loose goods.** Loose items went 681 (T15) to 1839 (T36) and stayed at 1824 after I added 238 stockpile cells (T34 to T35). Most of it is probably dig stone and the far-off gather and chop yield. I'm not sure if that is intended, but haulers seem to spend their time on it.
5. **Kiln "waiting for logs" with 636-968 logs in stock** (T29-T33). Its single input crate was empty most turns, while it shared hauling with everything else.
6. **Academy:** Ilmra was "making literate at the small lectern" after she had graduated (T31). Is she teaching the next student, or studying again? The report should say who the student is.

## 6. Balance

- **Logs are far too cheap and plentiful.** 191 in 12h. After one big chop order: 968 logs by day 19 and 1032 by day 23. The surplus mostly costs hauling time.
- **Fiber is the bottleneck for almost everything.** Beds, rushlights, thread, cloth, rugs, paper, books and all tier-2 home goods need it, and the only source is wild plants. There is no fiber crop. Gatherers walked 50-70 tiles (390,265; 275,272; 326,334). Four people on farming 1 got it to 174 by day 21. A flax/hemp crop, or fiber from wheat straw, would fix this.
- **Food is easy.** Glowcaps (3 days, eaten raw, any roofed floor) plus 13 days of starting food meant I never had to think about food. Bread came on day 11 and is a nice-to-have.
- **Size-1 stations cost 2 logs or 2 stone and are built in hours.** I never needed planks for anything big. 103 planks sat untouched from day 7.
- **Settlers:** 18 in 22 days in waves of 1-5, generous early. They stalled from day 19 with 11 free beds, 11 days of food and 60% mood, perhaps because of living standard.
- **Education:** a Literate costs books (3 paper + ink + cloth) + 2 paper, about 5 fiber. That feels right. The 2400-tick study time gave one Literate every 2.5 days.
- **Ants:** nest #8 at 252,407 grew from 7 ants (day 9) to 40 (day 21), all calm. No threat in 23 days, so I never tested combat.

## 7. Top suggestions

1. **Haulers clear the cells under blueprints, or the status names the blocker.** This bug silently stalled three rooms for 1.5 days, and underground floors are always covered in rubble.
2. **Assign crates to an item (or a machine), and show it** (`part output_crate X Y item=bread`, "Output crate (bread)"). Crate claiming caused four separate stalls.
3. **Production targets: "until N in stock" per station, plus pause.** Overproduction wasted fiber, cloth and paper, and idling a station by giving it an impossible recipe is a hack.
4. **Report unreachable work.** For example "Dig 95 (95 unreachable)", or idle colonists with a reason. This cost me day 1-3.
5. **Add a fiber crop.** Fiber gates beds, lighting, cloth, paper and education, and the wild supply ends up 60 tiles out.
6. **Split "in stock" into stored and lying loose per item, and make hauling serve crates first.** Chains starved for fiber, logs and paper that were "in stock".
7. **Explain living standard** (what raises it, what it gates). Show it per home, and say why settlers stop coming.

### Did the new report sections help?

- **Room status lines:** yes, a lot. "needs 2 output crates (one per product...)" and "waiting for fiber" told me what to fix most of the time. The exception was the blueprint stall, where "needs 2 logs and a builder" was misleading.
- **Crate contents:** essential. This is the only way I saw the crate-claim problem.
- **What homes still want:** excellent, clear and actionable (e.g. "7 bed of tier 1 (straw_pallet: 0 in stock)"). It also showed exactly what tier 2 needs.
- **Time spent per activity:** useful for spotting the idle problem (44%/73% idle) and the hauling squeeze (65%). It would be better with a reason for idling.
- **Missing:** which item each crate is assigned to, and "in stock" split into stored and lying loose.

## Follow-up (fixed after this playtest)

- Blueprints on cells with goods lying about: haulers now clear a blueprint's footprint first (to a stockpile, or
  beside it), so the material can be delivered. Test: a crate planned on rubble behind a hauling backlog is built.
- Output crates: a product may take an empty crate only while enough stay free for the room's other products that
  hold none (as input crates already did). Planks no longer take the furniture's crate.
- Fiber: a flax crop (farm plots on grass or soil, 3 days, 4 fiber a plot).
- Settlers stalled: couples had claimed 6- and 7-bed homes, whose spare beds are kept for children, leaving no free
  beds for settlers. Couples now claim homes of at most 4 beds; a settler wave is cancelled only when over a tenth of
  the colony is starving (was: anyone).
- Stations never stopped: machines now have a stock limit (Inspect: Keep in store; playtest order `limit X Y N`).
- The report shows each living-standard need and what meets it, the next wave and free beds for settlers, family
  homes, orders nobody can reach, and rock floor (+) in level views.
